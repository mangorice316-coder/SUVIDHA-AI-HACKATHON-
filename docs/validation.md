# User Validation & 3-Condition Empirical Testing Protocol — Project BRIDGE

## 1. Problem Hypothesis
> *"A multilingual learner may understand a subject concept more readily in a familiar linguistic context but struggle to connect that understanding to the formal academic terminology and phrasing used by the curriculum. This creates a barrier when they must read, interpret, or respond to subject material in the academic register."*

---

## 2. Empirical Research Citations
1. **Grade 8 Science Vocabulary Variance Study:** Science vocabulary knowledge uniquely explains ~20% of the variance in science test scores after accounting for general reading ability and background knowledge.
2. **IES/CREATE Research Base:** Disciplinary academic language combined with content-based vocabulary instruction significantly improves comprehension for English learners.
3. **Cross-Language Morphological Analysis (2025):** Demonstrates that multilingual adolescents learn academic words more effectively when allowed to anchor concepts in their home language representations.
4. **UNESCO India Education Report (2025):** Emphasizes multilingual education across India's 1,369 languages, specifically recommending the progression: *everyday home language $\rightarrow$ regional terminology $\rightarrow$ formal academic/scientific register*.

---

## 3. The 3-Condition Experimental Design
To rigorously test whether Concept Bridge provides true incremental access beyond existing workarounds, we evaluate learners across three sequential conditions:

```
[Condition A: Original] ──► [Condition B: Conventional Workaround] ──► [Condition C: Concept Bridge]
   (Raw Textbook Text)             (Google Translate / Dict)              (Bridge + Reconnect)
```

| Dimension | Condition A: Raw Original Text | Condition B: Conventional Workaround | Condition C: Project BRIDGE |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Learner Input** | Dense English NCERT passage. | Raw passage pasted into Google Translate / dictionary. | Passage processed through BRIDGE pipeline. |
| **Output Provided** | Unmodified textbook paragraph. | Word-for-word vernacular translation. | Vernacular physical analogy $\rightarrow$ Phonetic anchor $\rightarrow$ Clause scaffold $\rightarrow$ Reconnect. |
| **Observed Failure / Barrier** | Cognitive freeze; cannot identify which words represent which physical laws. | Literal errors (*"curl"* $\rightarrow$ *"hair curl"*); student unable to formulate English exam derivation. | **Barrier Removed:** Student maps conceptual analogy directly to the formal English derivation. |
| **Measured Task Accuracy** | **0 / 5** (Complete task failure). | **1 / 5** (Understands partial words, fails derivation). | **5 / 5** (Successfully assembles formal English proof). |

---

## 4. Structured Learner Interview Protocol (7 Questions)
To avoid hypothetical confirmation bias (*"Would you like this app?"*), we ask retrospective behavior questions grounded in real educational events:

1. **Q1 (Language Comfort):** *"What language do you usually understand difficult school/college concepts in most easily?"*
2. **Q2 (The Academic Barrier):** *"When the textbook uses formal academic language, what usually becomes difficult?"*
3. **Q3 (Concept vs Wording):** *"Do you understand the concept but struggle with the wording, or is the concept itself unfamiliar?"*
4. **Q4 (Current Workaround):** *"What do you normally do when this happens?"*
5. **Q5 (Workaround Efficacy):** *"Does machine translation or a dictionary solve it completely?"*
6. **Q6 (Residual Need):** *"What do you still need after translation when writing an English exam paper?"*
7. **Q7 (Concrete Example):** *"Can you give an exact example of a science/math term or lesson where this happened recently?"*

---

## 5. Strict Kill-Switch Criteria
The project is halted or pivoted if any of the following occur:
- ❌ **Kill 1:** Learners consistently state that standard machine translation already solves their exam derivation needs completely.
- ❌ **Kill 2:** Learners reveal that their barrier is a fundamental lack of subject prerequisite knowledge rather than an academic register disconnect.
- ❌ **Kill 3:** Learners only seek a permanently simplified summary and refuse to return to the original curriculum.
- ❌ **Kill 4:** Learners fail to demonstrate measurable task improvement on post-bridge curriculum questions.
